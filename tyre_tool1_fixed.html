3 · Rotation axis & working half

The rotation axis is the wheel/hub centreline — a horizontal line below the visible profile. It need not be at Y = 0: DXFs are often exported at arbitrary coordinates. The robust way to place it is the outer diameter — the tread surface (highest points in the drawing) sits at radius OD/2, so entering OD sets the axis to Y_tread − OD/2. Every point's Y-distance from this line is its true radius.
Pick Left or Right when you work one half of the section (the common case): assign only that side and the tool doubles it for the full tyre. A component that crosses the centreline (e.g. the crown wrapping over the top) is counted once, and flagged. Pick Both when both halves are drawn and you want to assign each side yourself (e.g. asymmetric grooves) — everything is counted once.

A clean DXF is the single biggest factor in getting accurate, error-free results. The tool detects closed regions from the section outline, so anything that leaves a region open — or adds stray geometry — throws off the calculation. Follow the checklist below when exporting from your CAD package.

1 · Draw only the tyre cross-section

  • Export a single half of the section (left or right of the centreline) whenever the tyre is symmetric — the tool doubles it for you. Export both halves only for asymmetric designs.
  • Delete everything that is not part of the section profile: title blocks, borders, dimension lines, leaders, centre-marks, hatching, text and construction geometry.
  • Keep the drawing in millimetres at 1:1 scale. Do not scale or rotate the section.

2 · Make every region close

  • Each component (tread, belts, plies, sidewall, bead, etc.) must be bounded by curves that meet end-to-end with no gaps and no overshoot.
  • Snap endpoints together in CAD before exporting. A gap of even a few microns leaves the region open — shown as a red dot in the viewer.
  • Shared boundaries between two components should be a single line, not two lines drawn on top of each other.

3 · Avoid overlapping and duplicate lines

  • Duplicate or partially overlapping segments confuse region detection and can double-count boundaries. Run your CAD's OVERKILL / purge-duplicates command before exporting.
  • In the viewer, press Overlaps in the toolbar to highlight any overlapping or coincident lines in orange, then remove them in the Clean up the drawing panel.

4 · Use supported entity types

  • Supported: LINE, LWPOLYLINE, POLYLINE, ARC, CIRCLE, and SPLINE.
  • Not supported: blocks/inserts (explode them first), hatches, dimensions, MTEXT, ellipses and 3D solids. Explode or delete these before exporting.
  • Save as a plain ASCII DXF (R2000 or later). Avoid binary DXF and DWG.

5 · Check before you compute

  1. Load the DXF and read the diagnostic bar on the viewer: it reports enclosed regions, open endpoints and overlaps.
  2. Resolve every red dot — either delete the stray curve or bridge the real gap.
  3. Set the rotation axis (wheel centreline) and, for a one-sided section, confirm Work on → Right so the section is correctly doubled.
  4. Assign each region to a component, set specific gravities, then Compute results.

Rule of thumb: if the viewer shows zero open endpoints and zero overlaps, and the region count matches the number of components you expect, the results will be reliable.
