Section 01

Overview & Executive Summary

The patent landscape for TBR rolling resistance and mileage is active and evolving. Between 2015 and 2024, the dataset contains 855 patents primarily targeting rolling resistance and 1,512 targeting mileage/wear, with 357 patents addressing both simultaneously. The picture that emerges is one of two parallel innovation streams — chemistry-driven for RR, structure-driven for mileage — that rarely converge. That convergence gap is arguably the biggest opportunity in the space.

Section 02

Rolling Resistance Patent Deep-Dive

Rolling resistance patents — those explicitly mentioning RR, fuel efficiency, hysteresis, heat generation, or energy loss — number 855 over the decade. The technical approach has shifted noticeably: the industry started the period with a chemistry-first mindset (silica, silane coupling) and is moving toward a thermal systems mindset where compound, structure, and geometry are considered together. This is a meaningful change in how engineers are framing the problem.

RR Patent Sub-Theme Trends Over Time
Annual count of RR patents mentioning each technology theme. Heat management's rise is the clearest trend signal.
Observation

Heat Management Rising Fast

Patents framing RR through heat buildup / thermal management grew from 11 (2015) to 28 (2024) — a 155% increase. This signals a shift from filler-centric to system-level thinking on energy loss.

Observation

Silica: Dominant but Plateauing

Silica-related RR patents grew through 2023 (33) but dipped in 2024 (16). The base silica + silane approach is maturing; incremental gains are harder to patent distinctively.

Signal

Belt Structure Entering RR Space

Nearly absent pre-2019 (1 patent, 2015), belt/cord patents within RR reached 28 in 2019 and 19 in 2024. Structural contribution to RR is being recognized and patented — a newer angle.

Geo Signal

China's Structural Shift

Chinese RR filings jumped from 11–16/year (2015–2018) to 28–49/year (2019–2023). More importantly, the nature shifted from tread pattern geometry to rubber compound formulations — a capability upgrade.

Geo Signal

Japan Retreating on Volume

Japanese RR filings peaked at 42 (2018) and fell to 18–24/year since 2022. Major Japanese OEMs appear to be protecting core technology as trade secrets rather than expanding their patent surface.

Watch List

Germany Re-engaging

Continental filed 7 German RR patents in 2024 — its highest single-year count — including a notably different patent combining RR optimization with electrical conductivity for EVs. Worth monitoring closely.

Section 03

Mileage & Tread Life Patent Deep-Dive

With 1,512 patents, mileage/wear is nearly twice as active as rolling resistance. The approaches are structurally different: while RR is dominated by compound chemistry, mileage innovation leans heavily on belt and cord construction, tread geometry, and material durability. Two distinct peaks — 2019 and 2023 — reflect separate market drivers: fuel economy regulation in the first wave, and fleet TCO / EU tire labelling pressure in the second.

Key Finding

Structure vs. Chemistry Divide

Belt/cord structure makes up 20.3% of mileage patents but only 13.9% of RR patents. Conversely, silica compounds feature in 26.1% of RR patents vs. 15.5% of mileage. The two problems are being solved by fundamentally different engineering toolboxes.

Key Finding

Heat is Underweighted for Mileage

Heat management accounts for 20% of RR patents but only 7.1% of mileage patents. Yet heat buildup degrades rubber and accelerates wear. The role of thermal management in tread life improvement is significantly under-explored.

Key Finding

Tread Pattern Now a Mileage Lever

Tread pattern patents for mileage grew from 2/year (2015–2018) to 10–15/year (2022–2024). Engineers are using groove geometry, block stiffness, and contact pressure distribution as wear management tools — not just compounding.

Section 06

Opportunity Areas & White Spaces

The following areas represent the clearest gaps between where the industry is investing and where future value will be created — based on patent density, filing trajectory, and underlying technical logic. These are ranked by immediacy of the commercial opportunity and current IP sparsity.

1

Heat-Driven RR + Wear System Optimization

Heat generation is the root cause of both rolling resistance and rubber degradation, yet almost no patent treats it as a unified system problem. The opportunity: compound + belt + geometry co-optimization targeting thermal equilibrium at operating conditions — not just 60°C tan-delta.

Very Low IP Density High Commercial Pull
2

AI/ML-Guided Compound Design for RR + Mileage

Only 2 RR and 4 mileage patents use AI or simulation. An ML-guided formulation engine that simultaneously optimizes tan-delta (RR proxy), abrasion index (wear proxy), and wet grip is technically feasible today and IP-free. The data to train such models exists inside any major tire R&D lab.

Near-Zero IP Density First Mover Advantage
3

EV-Specific TBR: RR + Load + NVH + Conductivity

One patent exists (Continental, 2024) addressing the EV-specific conflict between low-RR silica compounds (low conductivity) and the need for static charge dissipation. EV trucks add heavier load, higher torque, and zero-noise cabin requirements. No comprehensive EV-TBR system patent family exists.

1 Patent Worldwide Growing Market Pull
4

Contact Patch Pressure Homogeneity as an RR Lever

Tread pattern patents for RR are now appearing (23 patents) but largely from Chinese OEMs focused on groove geometry. The more sophisticated approach — using computational contact mechanics to design patterns that minimize pressure peaks and thus reduce deformation energy — has barely any coverage.

Sparse IP Landscape Differentiable Claim
5

Graphene as Synergist in Carbon Black Hybrid Systems

Graphene as a standalone filler failed (processing and cost). A 2023 Chinese university patent showed that small amounts of graphene synergize with structured carbon black during vulcanization to create a more efficient network — improving both service life and RR. This mechanism is patentable and almost unclaimed by industry.

1–2 Patents Lab-Stage, High Upside
6

Multi-Life (Retread-Optimized) Compound Design

Retreading extends TBR tire life by 40–50% at a fraction of new tire cost. Yet compound design explicitly targeting first-life wear uniformity + carcass adhesion preservation for retreadability is addressed in fewer than 5 patents. A compound engineered around the full TBR lifecycle — first life + retread life — is wide open.

Very Low IP Density Fleet TCO Play
7

Inner Liner as Active RR Contributor

Inner liner patents are 1.5% of RR filings — minimal, despite the liner's role in preventing air permeation (which affects pressure, contact patch, and therefore RR). Ultra-thin, low-permeability, high-flexibility liner materials designed explicitly as an RR lever, rather than a sealing component, is a framing that has not been claimed.

Low IP Density Incremental, Patentable
8

Low-VOC + Low-RR Tread Rubber (Dual Compliance)

One patent filed in 2025 in China targets low-VOC all-steel truck tread rubber. European cabin air quality regulations and sustainability mandates are converging. A tread compound achieving both low RR and low VOC emission (especially from process oils and plasticizers) addresses two regulatory pressures simultaneously — and the IP is effectively unclaimed.
