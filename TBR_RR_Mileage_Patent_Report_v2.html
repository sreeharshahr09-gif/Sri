Section 03

Rolling Resistance — Deep Dive

With 1,150 patents, the RR landscape is 35% larger than previously counted. The clearest structural finding: inventors describe RR through three distinct lenses — physics (loss tangent, tan delta, heat build-up), commercial outcomes (fuel consumption reduction), and material mechanisms (hysteresis, viscoelastic loss). Patents using each lens cluster differently by geography and company — understanding which vocabulary a competitor uses reveals their R&D framing.

RR Patent Keyword Group Trends (2015–2024)
Annual count of RR patents by the vocabulary cluster used. Heat/Hysteresis and Fuel Consumption are now the dominant signal — together larger than direct "rolling resistance" terminology.
Key Finding

Heat/Hysteresis Language Dominates

Patents using loss tangent, tan delta, heat build-up and related terms: 32→66 patents (2015→2023), overtaking direct RR language. Japanese and Korean OEMs drive this — they write compound-science patents, not application patents.

Key Finding

Fuel Consumption — China's Language

"Fuel consumption reduction" is the dominant framing in Chinese RR patents. At 17→51 (2015→2018) then 37→42 (2023–24), this group captures a fleet-commercial perspective that the compound-science vocabulary misses entirely.

Signal

Belt/Cord in RR: Structural Lens Growing

Belt/cord mentions within RR patents nearly tripled from 2015 (8) to 2023–24 (27–29). Engineers are treating tire construction — not just rubber chemistry — as an RR lever. This framing is underexplored in the patent literature.

Technology Sub-themes in RR Patents (n=1,150)
Heat/Hysteresis is now the #1 theme, ahead of silica. Fuel consumption is nearly equal — both capture energy-loss framing.
Technology Sub-themes in Mileage Patents (n=1,760)
Abrasion/wear compound dominates at 48%. Belt/cord is #2. The contrast with RR (chemistry vs structure) is stark.

Japan / Korea

Physics-First Vocabulary

Japanese and Korean OEMs predominantly use loss tangent, tan delta, rebound resilience, heat build-up — measurement and material science terms. A search limited to "rolling resistance" misses ~35–40% of their output.

China

Commercial Outcomes Vocabulary

Chinese filers — both established OEMs and new entrants — favour fuel consumption reduction, fuel-saving tire, energy-saving. They frame the same innovation as a fleet economy benefit, not a physics property.

Europe / USA

Regulatory / Sustainability Vocabulary

Continental, Michelin, and Goodyear increasingly use CO₂ emission, environmental performance, green tire, eco tire — driven by EU tire labelling regulation. This vocabulary has grown from 2022 onwards.

Section 04

Mileage & Tread Life — Deep Dive

The mileage dataset expands to 1,760 patents with the comprehensive keyword set. The most impactful additions: retread/retreadability (88 patents, entirely missed before), abrasion standalone (98 patents), and tread depth (44 patents, growing to 18/year by 2024). Retread is particularly significant for TBR — it represents a lifecycle efficiency play that is commercially important but IP-sparse.

Mileage Patent Keyword Group Trends (2015–2024)
Direct wear/abrasion terms and durability/life terms move together. Retread is a separate, lower-volume but growing category. Tread pattern/depth is the fastest growing group 2020–2024.
RR vs. Mileage — Technology Approach Comparison
Radar showing % of patents in each theme. The structural difference is clear: RR is chemistry/energy, Mileage is structure/abrasion.
Retread Patents — A TBR-Specific White Space
Retread patents by year and top assignee. 88 total; growing to 15 in 2024. Yokohama and Continental lead — major OEMs largely absent.
New Finding

Retread: 88 Patents, Largely Ignored by Major OEMs

Yokohama (10) and Continental (9) lead retread patent activity, followed by specialty firms Flexsys America (6) and Bridgestone Bandag (4). Sumitomo, Michelin, and Goodyear are near-absent despite retread being a major TBR fleet value lever.

New Finding

Tread Pattern as a Mileage Lever is Accelerating

Tread pattern/depth patents in mileage context grew from 4/year (2015–2018) to 16–18/year (2022–2024). Groove geometry, block stiffness, and contact pressure distribution are now being used to manage wear — not just compounding.

Key Contrast

Heat Management Underapplied to Mileage

Heat management terms appear in 31.3% of RR patents but only 8.6% of mileage patents. Thermal degradation of rubber is a primary driver of tread wear — yet this mechanism is rarely framed as a mileage innovation. A significant framing gap.

Section 07

Opportunity Areas & White Spaces

Based on patent density, filing trajectory, and the underlying technical and commercial logic, the following areas represent the clearest R&D opportunities. The comprehensive keyword analysis makes these gaps more defensible — we are not missing hidden coverage, these are genuine white spaces.

1

Thermal System Optimisation for RR + Mileage

Heat is the root cause of both energy dissipation (RR) and rubber degradation (wear). Yet no patent treats it as a unified thermal system — compound + belt construction + tread geometry co-optimised for thermal equilibrium at operating conditions. Heat management features in 31% of RR patents but only 9% of mileage patents. Closing this gap is the clearest technical frontier.

No System-Level Patents High Commercial Pull
2

EV-Specific TBR Design (RR + Conductivity + Heavy Load + Wear)

Electric trucks impose unique simultaneous demands: heavier weight from batteries (higher load → higher RR and faster wear), higher torque (accelerated shoulder wear), silent cabin (NVH compounds the RR problem), and static charge dissipation (conflicts with silica-based low-RR compounds). Continental's single 2024 patent is the only response. No comprehensive EV-TBR system patent exists.

1 Patent Worldwide Growing Market Urgency
3

AI/ML-Guided Compound Design for Dual RR + Mileage

Zero dual RR+Mileage patents use AI or simulation. The "magic triangle" (RR vs. wet grip vs. wear) is the classic multi-objective optimisation problem in tire compound design. ML-guided formulation using field wear data + lab tan-delta measurements to simultaneously optimise all three is technically feasible and entirely unpatented at the system level.

0 Dual Patents First-Mover Advantage
4

Retread-Optimised Compound Design (Multi-Life TBR)

88 retread patents exist but only 8 address both RR and mileage. The compound design problem for multi-life TBR is different from single-life: wear uniformity in first life, carcass adhesion preservation for retread interface, consistent compound properties for tread bonding. No patent claims this multi-life compound optimisation framework. Yokohama and Continental are active but their scope is narrow.

8 Dual Patents Only Fleet TCO Impact
5

Contact Patch Pressure Homogeneity as RR Lever

Tread pattern patents for RR grew from near-zero to 27 (2015–2024), mostly from Chinese OEMs using empirical groove geometry. The more precise approach — computational contact mechanics to design tread blocks and groove angles that minimise peak pressure gradients, reducing deformation energy — has no patent coverage. This bridges simulation and tread design in a way that is novel and defensible.

Sparse — No Systematic Patents Differentiable Claim
6

Graphene as Network Modifier in CB Hybrid Systems

Graphene as a standalone filler peaked in 2021 and collapsed. A 2023 Chinese university patent showed graphene + multi-grade carbon black synergy during vulcanisation — improved service life and reduced hysteresis through network densification, not bulk filling. This bypasses the dispersion problem. The mechanism is novel, patentable, and almost entirely unclaimed by the industry.

1–2 Patents High Technical Upside
7

Low-VOC + Low-RR Tread Compound (Dual Compliance)

One patent (China, 2025) targets low-VOC all-steel truck tread rubber. EU vehicle cabin air quality regulation and tire sustainability mandates are converging. A tread compound achieving both low RR (low tan-delta) and low VOC emission (choice of process oil, plasticiser, and antiozonant system) addresses two regulatory pressures with one technical solution — and the IP window is wide open.

1 Patent Only Pre-Regulatory Window
8

Belt Cord Design Linking RR and Fatigue Life Simultaneously

Belt/cord patents appear in 15% of RR patents and 19% of mileage patents — but almost never as a deliberate co-optimisation. Belt flexibility affects both rolling energy loss and belt edge separation fatigue. High-elongation M+N cord structures (one 2024 HS Hyosung patent) start to address this. A broad system claim linking cord geometry parameters to simultaneous RR and durability targets is an open territory.
